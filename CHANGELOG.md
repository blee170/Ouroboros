# Changelog

All notable changes to Ouroboros, newest first. Every entry names a GitHub Release (`v<version>`), and a version without a release is said so. Dates are the day the version was tagged (KST). The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

Each version lists what changed under **Added**, **Changed**, **Fixed**, **Security**, and what the release was **verified** with (the automated gate runs typecheck, build and the vitest suite on every push; "built-app probe" means a Playwright run against the packaged app; "not verified" is said when a feature was only unit-tested).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Ouro: work the board from the car through Discord.** Settings → *Voice & phone* → *Ouro (Discord)* (off by default) connects a Discord bot you make, so you can talk to the board in a direct message: Android Auto reads the bot's messages aloud and takes a spoken reply, and the same works by typing on the phone. Say *상황 어때* / *status* to hear how many cards work and the first thing waiting on you (a card's question, a merge the action gate holds, a task that needs your OK or your hands, a card at a permission prompt), *다음* / *next* for the one after, *허가* / *approve* or *보류* / *hold* for the one you just heard, or a question's choice by name or number. Before anything is decided, Ouro reads it back word for word and acts only when the whole reply is a yes (*응*); any other reply, or a no anywhere in it, cancels, and so does anything else Ouro says first. A command or request that is long or has a link is not approved by voice (read it at the PC or on the phone; hold still works), a task only you can do is sent to the PC, and permission prompts are answered at the PC or on the phone page. Anything else you say goes to the Conductor as a message from you asking for a short spoken answer, and its next three replies within 20 minutes are read back. When something new waits on you, Ouro says so, at most once a minute (switch off in Settings); a switch also turns voice approvals off. Only direct messages from your Discord user ID are read; server channels and other bots are ignored. The bot token is stored encrypted and sent only to Discord. Every approval, hold, answer and message to the Conductor goes in the run log as "Ouro (Discord)". No dependencies: the app speaks Discord's gateway itself and reconnects on its own (a refused token or a switched-off Message Content Intent stops it with the reason in Settings).

Built app with a temporary profile connected to the real bot: ready, greeting sent, the token stored encrypted (not in settings.json).
- **Not verified live**: a spoken conversation through Android Auto (the DM test was not run); whether Android Auto reads a message the bot sends first. A Codex or Gemini Conductor that is mid-turn may have an unrelated reply read back.

### Changed
- **Settings is a section list instead of one long scroll.** The dialog is wider, with the sections listed on the left in four groups (General: Engines, Window & sessions, Announcements · Voice & phone: Voice input, Phone · Integrations: GitHub, Notion, Obsidian, Grok Bot · Advanced: WSL2 cards, Updates, About) and one section shown at a time. A search box above the list keeps only the sections whose text, hints or placeholders contain what you type and shows them together; Esc clears the search first, then closes. The status line ("Token saved", "Notion: found database…") and Close now stay at the bottom whatever section is open. Reopening Settings returns to the section used last. Every setting and what it does is unchanged, and a half-typed key survives switching sections.

## [0.14.0] — 2026-10-26

### Added
- **What a task needs from you, in plain words, with a button.** A card that blocks a task on you now says what it needs (`task_update owner_action`), written for someone who does not code, and the Tasks and Today tabs show it as a guide instead of a one-line reason. **Needs your OK** (a card does it once you say yes): the request and what the card will do, *Approve* (the task goes back to its card, which is messaged `APPROVED BY OWNER` and, if idle, woken to carry on) or *Not now* (it stays blocked, on hold, and the card is told not to). **Only you can do this** (production database or migration, money, accounts, secrets): numbered steps naming the window and the button, the exact text to paste with a *Copy* button, how to check it worked, a box to paste what you saw, and *Done, I did it* (the task is done and the card is messaged with what you pasted, to check it and carry on); there is no Approve on these. A note goes with any answer. Older tasks with only an `APPROVAL NEEDED:` reason get *Approve*, *I did it myself* and *Not now*. You no longer need to type `APPROVED BY OWNER` to the Conductor. Text boxes in an open task grow with what they hold. Your answer goes to the card that asked, even if another card touches the task later; an answer to a request that changed after you read it is refused (read it again); long runs of blank lines and blank-looking characters are taken out of the text to copy, so nothing hides below a harmless start; what you paste back goes in full only to that card (the task keeps one line). An approval covers what the task asked and nothing else; the action gates and Claude Code's own checks still apply.

### Verified (owner actions)
- Unit tests (input checks and limits, invisible characters, blocked-only, the asker kept, dropped on claim or a new reason, the board file, task_list, Today, the three messages); built-app probe with a stand-in `claude`: a guide per waiting task in Tasks and Today, Copy puts the exact SQL on the clipboard, Approve sends the task back to its card (doing) with an `APPROVED BY OWNER` message that wakes the idle card through its inbox, Done marks it done and messages the pasted output, Not now holds it and tells the card, a do task cannot be approved, a changed request is refused.
- **Not verified live**: a real Conductor acting on the approval message and writing good owner actions on its own.

### Fixed
- **Esc during a card's question.** Pressing Esc (or Ctrl+C) while a Claude card waited on `ask_owner` left the call waiting in the board's MCP server, which Claude Code does not tell, so an answer you gave in the next 45 seconds went nowhere. When the card does not take an answer within 10 seconds, it now gets it again as a board message from you.
- **A card you interrupted stayed "working".** Claude Code runs no hook when a turn is interrupted, so the card showed working until its next prompt and board mail waited for a turn end that never came. The card now goes idle ("interrupted") when its interrupt line shows within 5 seconds of the key, and mail wakes it as usual.

### Verified
- Live, with a real Claude Code 2.1.310 card on this machine (built app, temporary profile and project): an idle card woken by an owner message through its inbox, nothing typed into its terminal; `ask_owner` answered within the wait (the call returned the answer and note) and after it (the call returned `pending`, the late answer arrived as a message and woke the card); Esc during the wait, then an answer (the card went idle and got the answer by message); a message to a working card used in its reply. `~/.claude/settings.json` unchanged before and after. Unit tests for the interrupt key and line.
- **Not verified live**: Codex and Gemini cards calling `ask_owner`; the inbox on macOS and Linux.

## [0.13.0] — 2026-10-26

### Added
- **Protect main on GitHub.** Settings → *GitHub* → *Check* reads, with your own `gh` sign-in, whether the open project's default branch needs a pull request and refuses force pushes and deletion (rulesets only; classic branch protection is not read). *Protect…* then *Add ruleset* adds one ruleset, "Ouroboros · protect main": a pull request is required (0 approvals, so you can merge your own), force pushes and deletion are refused, and nobody bypasses it, you included (pushing straight to main stops working; merging a pull request does not). Nothing is sent when the branch is already protected, and a ruleset by that name that you switched off or edited is left alone. Private repositories need GitHub Pro. What it does not stop: a pull request merged by anyone who can push, a card token included. During autonomous runs the action gate asks you before `gh pr merge`, but a merge through the API (`curl`) is not seen.
- **Card-only GitHub token.** Settings → *GitHub* → *Card token*: a fine-grained token you make for the repositories cards work in (Contents and Pull requests read and write, with an expiry), stored encrypted. Agent cards started after you save it use it for `gh` (GH_TOKEN) and for `git push` (two runtime git config entries clear the github.com credential helpers, Git Credential Manager included, and answer with the token), so a card pushes its branches and opens pull requests as that token and the ruleset stops it at main. *Check* says whose it is and when it expires. Terminal cards keep your own sign-in, and so do agent cards while no token is saved. Only https remotes use it: a `git@github.com` remote pushes with your SSH key. Same OS user: a card that sets out to can still call the credential manager itself; this stops mistakes and stray pushes, not a determined card.
- **Cards ask you, and get the answer back.** Every agent card (mail-only ones too) has `ask_owner`: a question with 2 to 4 answers to pick from, or none for *Approve* / *Deny*. It shows in Today's **Needs you** with a button per answer and an optional note, and on the phone under **Questions**; a desktop notification fires at most once a minute per card. The card's call waits up to 45 seconds: answer by then and it is the call's result; a later answer reaches the card as a board message from you headed `[owner decision D<n>]`, and the card is told to carry on meanwhile. During an autonomous run the call returns at once, the question is listed under *Waiting on you* in the morning report, and answered ones get a *Decisions* section. One open question per card; it expires after 24 hours (the card is told) and goes away with its card. Questions are kept in memory per window, like approvals: a restart of the app drops them.
- **What a card is doing, on the card.** A Claude card's status line shows the step its current tool call announces (a shell command's own description, the file it edits or reads, the host of a page it fetches, the MCP tool it calls) instead of `tool: X`, and the title Claude Code gave the conversation shows as its topic: in the far-zoom summary, the Roster's search and tooltip, and Today's *Working now*. Nothing is asked of the agent. The topic is read from the new lines of the session's transcript at the end of each turn, only under the Claude config folder. Codex and Gemini cards have no live tool hooks and show what they showed before.
- **WSL2 cards (experimental). Inspector → *Run in WSL2 with the sandbox* starts a Claude card's `claude` inside a Linux distribution with Claude Code's sandbox on (`sandbox.enabled`, `failIfUnavailable`, no unsandboxed retry): its shell commands write only in the card's folder (not its git hooks, git config, Claude settings, `.mcp.json` or the board file) and cannot read Windows' own programs, the WSL interop socket or the credentials under your Windows home (SSH keys, gh, git, cloud CLIs, Claude, Ouroboros). Native Windows has no Claude sandbox. Before each launch, and from Settings → *WSL2 cards*, the app checks the distribution (Claude Code, bubblewrap and socat, the sign-in, and that the card can reach the app on 127.0.0.1, which needs mirrored networking) and says what to do for anything missing. The card's folder is reached through /mnt/c; only the card's own variables cross into Linux.

### Changed
- **Prompts go into a Claude card's own inbox.** Claude Code gives the MCP servers it starts an inbox; each card's board server hands it to the app, which then puts prompts, handoffs and board-mail nudges for that card there instead of typing them into its terminal: a working card takes them between tool calls, an idle one starts a turn, and nothing is pasted over what you are typing. When the inbox does not take a prompt (a closed session) it is typed in as before. The inbox token stays in memory, never in a file or the log. Codex and Gemini cards are still typed into.
- **Agents answering agents stop.** Board mail between cards stops after 8 messages in one thread, or 12 between two cards in 10 minutes, without a word from you; the sender is told to ask you instead (`ask_owner`). `send_message` now says when the recipient sees the message (woken now, after its next tool call, when it is started again, …).
- **Codex cards take part in autonomous runs.** They are restarted instead of closed when a run starts, and the Conductor can create them during a run. Every Codex launch while the run is on is forced into Codex's sandbox: workspace-write with no network and no extra writable folders, never asking (plan stays read-only), and the card's extra arguments are dropped. In that sandbox `.git` is read-only, so commits and pushes stay with Claude cards. Gemini and Grok cards are still closed. Outside a run, Codex auto mode now passes the documented `-s workspace-write -a on-request` instead of `--full-auto`.
- **A reset of the local Supabase stack is allowed during a run.** With the action gate installed, `supabase db reset` (also through `npx`) is decided by the gate instead of denied: allowed with no flags beyond `--local`, `--no-seed`, `--debug`, `--yes` and `--version`, refused with `--linked`, `--db-url`, any other flag or options before the subcommand. `db push` and migrations stay denied, and no paid branching is used.

### Verified
- Unit tests: the inbox path check and frames, a push into a real named pipe and the failure when nobody listens, the inbox route; the mail loop guard (thread depth, one direction, the owner's word resets it, replies only by a party) and the delivery note; the step taken from each tool's input, control and bidi characters, the title line in a transcript chunk, the transcript path check; ask_owner input checks, one question per card, an answer handed to the waiting call, a caller that hangs up, expiry, withdrawal on removal and project switch, the Today row, the report's Waiting on you and Decisions, the phone and hook routes, the MCP tool.

- **Not verified live**: a real Claude card in the app taking prompts through its inbox (the probe used a stand-in), an answer given after the 45 s sent as a board message (unit-tested), Esc on a waiting ask_owner in real Claude Code, the inbox socket name on macOS/Linux (only Windows was checked), and Codex/Gemini cards calling ask_owner (their MCP tool time limits were not tested).

## [0.12.0] — 2026-10-26

### Added
- **Hand tasks to Grok Bot.** Agent cards (the Conductor, and cards with board tools) get `handoff_to_grok_bot`: they can ask to send a task, with optional context, to Grok Bot, xAI and Cursor's cloud teammate with its own browser and computer, through one of your routines' webhook. Nothing leaves the machine on a card's say-so: each handoff waits in the **Today** tab (ranked right after approvals, with a notification) until you open *Review*, which shows the whole task and context and the routine it goes to, and press *Send to Grok Bot* (off until you have reviewed it) or *Discard*, and the card is told by board mail either way (with Grok Bot's run id when sent). A failed send (wrong key, routine switched off, network) stays waiting with the reason, so you can fix it and send again. Settings → *Grok Bot*: the routine's webhook URL (only `https://api2.cursor.sh/automations/webhook/…` is accepted, since the key goes with it), its key (stored encrypted), and *Test*, which starts one run with a body that says it is a test. The routine's instruction decides where the result goes (for example your Notion Tasks database); it does not come back as a board message. Limits: 4000 characters of task, 12000 of context, 10 waiting at once and 3 per card, the same task from the same card once, one notification per card a minute; text with invisible or direction-changing characters is refused, so what you review is what is sent. This gates the tool, not the machine: a card that can run shell commands could still send data out on its own, as before.

### Verified
- Unit tests (webhook URL check, handoff limits and duplicates, the body the routine receives, the Today ranking, the POST and every answer: a run started, 200 without one, routine off, wrong key, other statuses, network; no key in any message); built-app probe with a stand-in `claude`: a terminal card and a caller without a card id are refused, an agent card's handoff waits and is listed in Today with the badge, Send without a webhook says what to set and leaves it waiting, Send stays off until *Review* is open and the review shows the context, Discard removes it and mails the card, Test without a URL says so.
- **Not verified against a real Grok Bot routine** (no Grok plan on this machine): that a run starts with this body and that the routine sees its fields.

## [0.10.0] — 2026-10-26

### Added
- **Grok Build as a fourth engine.** A card can run xAI's `grok` CLI next to Claude Code, Codex and Gemini: *New card → Grok agent*, or Inspector → Engine → *Grok Build (xAI)*, or the Conductor's `create_agent engine=grok`. It gets the same status, lines, reports and board tools: a per-card plugin folder (passed with `--plugin-dir`, so `~/.grok` is never touched) carries its lifecycle hooks (`SessionStart`, `UserPromptSubmit`, `PreToolUse`, `Stop`, `StopFailure`, `Notification`, `SessionEnd`, run through `agent-hook.js` with the card's token file) and the board MCP server (mail tools only unless board tools are on). Model: *Grok 4.7* or any name `grok models` lists; *auto* and *bypass* permission modes start it with `--always-approve`; Plan and Accept edits use Grok's own default. The role prompt goes in with `--rules` on posix shells and as the first message on Windows. Settings → Engines detects it (installed, signed in with `grok login` or `XAI_API_KEY`) and shows `irm https://x.ai/cli/install.ps1 | iex`. `XAI_API_KEY` stays with Grok cards only; Grok cards lose the other engines' keys. Grok cards need Node.js on `PATH` and, like Codex and Gemini, are closed when an autonomous run starts. Grok needs a SuperGrok / X Premium+ plan or an xAI API key.
- **Obsidian vault export.** Settings → *Obsidian*: pick your vault folder and turn on *Mirror this project into my Obsidian vault*. The open project is written under `Ouroboros/<project>/` in the vault a few seconds after its tasks or cards change (and with *Export now*): one note per task in `Tasks/` (frontmatter: id, title, state, owner as the card's title, parent and dependencies as links, created, updated, tags `ouroboros/task` and `ouroboros/<state>`; the detail, result and reason as the body), the morning reports in `Reports/` with a date, `Board.canvas` (the board as an Obsidian canvas: each card with its engine, state and last reply, coloured by state; browser cards as web links; lines as arrows), `Tasks.base` (an Obsidian Bases table grouped by state, and cards) and a home note that embeds it. Obsidian's graph then draws the tasks and their links. It is one-way and keeps your vault yours: nothing outside that folder is written; a file you edit in Obsidian, or one that was there before, is left alone from then on (a manifest in the folder keeps a hash of what the app wrote); a task's note is removed only when the task is gone and the note is unchanged; an export with nothing new writes nothing. The folder belongs to one project (a second project with the same folder name is refused, not mixed in); morning reports are never removed; a damaged manifest stops the export with the reason instead of guessing; a network path is refused as the vault. Text written by agents (task details, replies, reports) is made inert for plugins that run code from notes: Templater `<% %>`, Dataview query blocks and inline queries.

### Changed
- **Model is a dropdown.** A card's Inspector → Model offers the engine's presets (Claude: Opus, Sonnet, Haiku as the latest of each, Fable 5.1, and pinned 5.5 ids), the CLI's default, or *Custom…* for any name; Codex and Gemini get default or custom (their own `/model` lists the names). Switching a card's engine resets a model name that belonged to the old one.
- **A new Briefs card opens beside the card whose reply had the link**, top edges aligned (the next free spot below in that column if something is there), instead of the first free grid cell, often far down-left. A Briefs card that already exists stays where you put it.
- **Announcements only when you are needed.** A Conductor reply used to be announced ("finished, please check the report") after every turn of 10 s or more, progress updates included. Now, by default, a finished turn is announced only when the reply asks for a decision or hands over a report: it ends with an `OWNER: DECIDE — …` or `OWNER: REPORT — …` line (the Conductor's prompt tells it to add one only in those two cases), it has a claude.ai artifact link, it says APPROVAL NEEDED, or the card blocked a task on your approval during the turn. The spoken sentence says which ("needs your decision" / "a report is ready"), and the toast shows the owner line. "All cards have finished" is no longer announced in this mode. A card waiting on a permission prompt and a failed turn are announced as before. Settings → *Announce* → *When* → *every reply* brings back the old behaviour. A Conductor started before this version does not have the owner-line rule in its prompt: restart it once.

### Verified

- Briefs placement: unit tests (beside, below a card in the way, the next column); built-app probe: a brief opened from a card lands right of it (and below its neighbour when the right is taken).
- Grok: unit tests (plugin folder, launch line per shell, hook mapping, sign-in, key stripping, autonomy, presets) and the bundled hook helper; built-app probe with a stand-in `grok` on PATH that runs the plugin's hooks as documented: detection, the card reaching idle from its SessionStart hook, a prompt's reply arriving through its Stop hook, and the board MCP listing only the mail tools.

## [0.9.0] — 2026-10-25

### Added
- **Board mail between cards** (RESEARCH §11 step 2). Every agent card (Claude, Codex, Gemini) gets three MCP tools, `send_message` (to a card id or exact title, kind *status / question / worker_done / escalation / note*, optional `reply_to`), `check_inbox` and `ack`. A card without board tools gets a server with only these three and its own mail token (a third HMAC domain, good only for `/api/mail*` and only as that card); cards with board tools keep their set plus mail. Who may write to whom is code: you and the Conductor reach any card, a worker reaches its parent, the Conductor and cards it shares a handoff line with. Limits: 8000 characters, 50 unread per recipient, 10 a minute per sender, the same text twice within a minute is refused.
